A task runs from its prompt to the last event before the next prompt; the open task runs to the last event recorded. Model time is the sum of the gaps before each of the model's messages; tool time the sum of each tool's run; the two overlap nowhere and do not add up to the wall time (the harness, hooks and the reading of results take the rest).

What is measured, and what is not

  • Wall time is the honest number for "how long did it take": prompt to last event. It includes the time Josu spent reading and typing between the model's messages within a task, which is small here, and the waits for tools.
  • Model time sums the gap before each message of the model: thinking and writing, with the harness's round trips inside it. Tool time sums each tool's run from call to result: shell commands, file reads and writes, the Quarto render, the LuaLaTeX builds (one of them, a 600 s timeout, is most of T3's tool time).
  • Tokens are the model's own counts: written (thinking apart), read from the cache, written to the cache. Cost follows from them at the model's prices (I13).
  • Not measured: the time before the first prompt (Josu's extraction of the zip, his reading of the files), and anything done outside this session (the opening of the app, a later session: it would be a second block on this page).

Why from the transcript

A timestamp typed at the start and the end of a task is a second record that drifts from the first: it is forgotten, rounded, or written after the fact. The transcript is written anyway, to the millisecond, for every event, and it holds more than two stamps: where the time went (model, tools, waiting), what it was spent on (which tools, how many times), how much was written, and when the brief changed mid-task. One script reads it and the page follows. The same script, pointed at another project's transcripts, benchmarks that project (I13).

On this page

    What this is

    The working site of the ESRF ID22 measurements proposed by Irene Urcelay-Olabarria (proposal HC-6528, sample 400_4), taken by Irene, Iñigo González de Arrieta and Mireia Sainz-Menchón: 101 powder diffraction scans from 900 to 1300 °C kept exactly as received, copied under names that say what they are, the figures of their evolution, and everything the team needs to interpret them, logged step by step. Josu M. Igartua treats the data.
