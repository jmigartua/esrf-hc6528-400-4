How to use them

  • The format. Plain text, three columns per line: 2θ in degrees (0.001° step to 26.999°), intensity in counts after the beamline's normalisation, estimated standard deviation. No header. The first 0.02° is the beamstop's shadow (zero intensity, large esd). See Data: file format.
  • The names. hc6528_400_4_Qu_<seq>_T<temp>C_t<elapsed>s_<long|short>.xye: sequence, nominal temperature, seconds since the first scan, exposure class. The scheme and the original names: Data: naming.
  • In any program. Every powder-diffraction program reads three-column .xye (Topas, GSAS-II, FullProf through its XY formats, Python with numpy.loadtxt).
  • In diff-ref-struct. Unpack the scans into a folder Data/ and the sets zip beside it; drs plots open Plots/hold-1270.drv reads a set, drs-publish build Publish/esrf-series.drp remakes the projections, the app's Plots module opens any .drv with its Projection, Map and Surface views. The maps need the scans rebinned by the core; the README in the zip gives the two commands.
  • Check what you got. The SHA-256 of every file is in the index and in the table; shasum -a 256 <file> on a Mac, sha256sum on Linux.
On this page

    What this is

    The ESRF ID22 measurements proposed by Irene Urcelay-Olabarria (proposal HC-6528, sample 400_4) and taken by Irene, Iñigo González de Arrieta and Mireia Sainz-Menchón: 101 powder diffraction scans from 900 to 1300 °C kept exactly as received, copied under names that say what they are, the figures of their evolution, and what the files show. Josu M. Igartua treats the data so that the team can interpret them.

    Irene Urcelay-Olabarria (proposer) · Iñigo González de Arrieta & Mireia Sainz-Menchón (experimental team, with Irene) · Josu M. Igartua (data treatment)
